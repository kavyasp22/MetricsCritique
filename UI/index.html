2Alerts (False Discovery Rate-gated)feeds the notification service

The highest-value section: which cells are behaving unusually enough to act on, and the exact evidence behind each one.

Alert severity

Two severity levels are produced: ▲high and ●medium. Topic alerts: high if q < 0.01, otherwise medium. Entity, risk and theme alerts: high only if q < 0.01 and at least 10 posts that day and at least 2× the baseline; otherwise medium. All alerts have q < 0.05. No alert is ever given a “critical” level, and there is no “low” level.
How a candidate becomes an alert click to expand

This is the logic the notification service will consume, so it is spelled out rather than buried. Each cell's volume is tested against a Negative-Binomial baseline fitted on its own trailing window, giving an exceedance probability. Those p-values are corrected across every supported cell within the same day by Benjamini–Hochberg, and alert_flag is q < 0.05 — a calibrated false-discovery rate, not an arbitrary z threshold. Cells that fail the support gate are never tested at all, so they cannot be flagged.

Severity is evidence strength, not impact — a very small q-value on a four-post cell is strong evidence of a small thing. Colour never carries the severity alone: every chip pairs an icon with the word.

Alert candidates per day

Alert candidates

Sorted by severity, then by q-value. The rationale column is the human-readable trace of why the cell fired — it is carried in alert_candidates.rationale so the notification payload can quote it verbatim.

Tone mix by platform provisional

About this panel click to expand

How many scored posts are positive, neutral or negative, per platform, never pooled across platforms (platforms differ too much: net scores range from about −70 to +60). A post is positive if its tone is above +0.15 and negative if below −0.15. Tone reads the wording, not the poster’s attitude: a factual report of a bad event is scored negative, so a low value can mean negative news rather than a hostile audience. The net sentiment score is 100 × (positive share − negative share). Audience-weighted tone gives posts with more views and interactions more influence, with weight 1 + ln(1 + views + likes + comments + shares), so a post with no counter still counts once; where a platform has no counters it equals the plain average. Provisional: the tone model is not yet human-validated. In a first hand-labelled check it scored factual reports of bad events as negative, so these numbers reflect news content as much as opinion, and the ±0.15 cut-off is not validated per language.

Largest tone drops provisional

About this panel click to expand

Days where a topic's tone on a platform fell significantly below its own previous 14 days (today is not in the baseline). z compares today with the baseline mean in baseline standard deviations (floor 0.05); q is the false-discovery-controlled probability across all topic × platform series that day. Needs at least 5 scored posts today and on at least 7 of the previous 14 days. This is a monitor and does not feed the alert list.

Reach proxy

About this panel click to expand

No follower or subscriber counts exist anywhere in the corpus. The bars below show observed views only (platforms with a view counter). Cross-Platform Narrative Diffusion multi-platform

About this panel click to expand

Tracks narratives that appear with identical text on two or more platforms. Observed views are counter values summed over platforms that report views; nothing is estimated. Interactions on posts without a view counter are shown separately and are not converted into views. The “Who Posted” column marks stories where the account name is the same on every platform: that is one account re-publishing its own text (cross-posting), not spread between different accounts; most stories are of this kind. “Earliest observed” is the first post we collected, which depends on scrape lag.

Shared-Link Narratives preliminary

About this panel click to expand

Preliminary. A second, separate way to link posts: two or more platforms posting the same article link (tracking parameters removed). It finds stories that exact-text matching misses, such as a translated post or a different headline for the same article. It only covers posts that contain a link, shows observed views only (no estimates), and is not merged into the narratives above. Precision was judged by hand by one reviewer on 100 links (not independently reviewed): full article links 35 of 40 judged right, shortened links 48 of 48. All 5 wrong full links were reference or app links whose posts were 26 days or more apart, so such links (posts more than 21 days apart) are now low confidence and listed separately. High = a full article link, posts within 21 days; medium = a shortened link, where the destination is not visible and most cases are one news aggregator posting the same item on two platforms, so treat them as one source, not independent spread.

Cross-Platform Events (Semantic) preliminary

About this panel click to expand

Preliminary. Links posts on different platforms that say the same thing in different words or languages, using the stored BGE-M3 meaning embeddings: cosine similarity at least 0.85, posted within 3 days of each other, identical text excluded (that is the exact-text table above). Connected posts form an event. Unlike the exact-text table, most events here involve different accounts, so they show real spread of one story. Precision was judged by hand by one reviewer on 100 events (not independently reviewed): high 45 of 45 and medium 42 of 44 judged right; 9 rows were marked unsure because the reviewer could not read the language, and one of the 2 wrong ones was a Dhivehi post matched to an unrelated English one. Similarity below 0.80 was not tested. High = every link in the event is at least 0.90; medium = some links are 0.85 to 0.90. Events with more than 30 distinct texts are marked large: they may merge several related stories. Observed views only, no estimates.

17Risk categories & thematic dimensionsverification: PARTIAL

9 risk categories and 12 thematic dimensions, multi-label per post via zero-shot embedding similarity to a category description — shown here, flagged here, because the fix is re-tuning, not silence.

⚠ Verification: PARTIAL — read the shape, not the label boundaries, as ground truth

Labels come from a two-step method: a bge-m3 similarity screen proposes candidates, then a multilingual NLI model (mDeBERTa) decides each (post, category) pair with one global threshold of 0.22 shared by all 9 risk and 12 theme categories — there is no per-category calibration. The script's own 105-post calibration claimed about 72% precision, but a later hand check of the assigned labels found only about 40% (risk) and 31% (theme) clearly right, and 70% / 55% counting loose matches. It replaced an earlier flat-cosine version (0.45) that measured 29% / 45% precision and is no longer in use.

Only 31.2% of the corpus (155,369 of 497,530 posts) carries any risk or theme label, and recall is still limited. Treat risk_labels/theme_labels as a screening signal, not ground truth — filter to gray_zone=false for stricter use, and re-tune thresholds per category before relying on single labels.
